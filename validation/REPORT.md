- Source `nrel-denver-yield`: NREL PVWatts v6 run for Denver, CO (39.74 N, 104.98 W), 1 kWdc fixed open rack, 40 degree tilt, 180 degree azimuth, 14 % system losses
- Source `pvwatts-v5`: Dobos, 'PVWatts Version 5 Manual', NREL/TP-6A20-62641 (2014)
- Source `bird-hulstrom-1981`: Bird & Hulstrom, 'A simplified clear sky model for direct and diffuse insolation on horizontal surfaces', SERI/TR-642-761 (1981)
- Source `duffie-beckman`: Duffie & Beckman, Solar Engineering of Thermal Processes, 4th ed. — Ch. 1 (extraterrestrial radiation), Ch. 2 (Hay-Davies transposition, air mass)

Twelve representative days, the 15th of each month, weighted by the length of the month. Horizontal irradiance lands on the published annual mean. The array then over-produces, and the whole of the excess is tilt gain: the model's in-plane insolation is 1.22 times its own horizontal, where the PVWatts run implies about 1.10. A synthetic cloud series with no seasonal structure leaves the model too many clear winter days, and a clear winter day at a 40 degree tilt collects nearly twice what the horizontal does. The model also has no soiling and no snow.
